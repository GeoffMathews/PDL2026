Using the Cepheid Period-Luminosity relation

Read the following text and watch the video to review how to determine the luminosity of a Cepheid star, one of our most important tools for measuring the distances to nearby galaxies.  

Cepheid stars are one of our most useful tools for measuring large distances.  They have a strict pattern between their period (how much time they take to go through a cycle of changing brightness) and their average luminosity (how bright they are, instrinsically).  So just by watching one over time, we can figure out how bright it is.  By comparing its luminosity to how bright it appears, we can figure out its distance, and thus the distance to whatever group of stars we find it in (whether a globular cluster in our own galaxy, or a nearby galaxy).  

Longer period Cepheid stars have higher luminosities.  The brightest ones can be seen hundreds of millions of light-years away - and within that distance, there are more than a few thousand large galaxies and tens of thousands of small galaxies, so Cepheid stars allow us to closely study the full range of galaxies and their interactions.   This pattern between period and luminosity is shown in the graph below.
