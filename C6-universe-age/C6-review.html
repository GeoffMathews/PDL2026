Calculating Age of the Universe

Read the following text and watch the video to review how to use a Hubble plot to calculate the age of the universe.

We can use just a pair of numbers from a Hubble plot to estimate the age of the entire Universe.

  1. Approximate that a distant galaxy has always been carried away from us at the speed we measure now (it has not, but this allows a simple calculation that will get us close to the result of more thorough calculations).
  2. Divide its distance by that approximated speed to calculate approximately how long ago that galaxy was near us. Since it was nearby when the universe formed, and is now far away, that tells us how long ago in the past the universe formed, i.e., its age.

Use the Cosmological redshift simulator (opens in new window) to develop an intuitive sense between the slope of the Hubble plot and the age of the universe.

  • set n-galaxies to 2 or 3, making a smaller simulated universe that is easier to examine for patterns
  • activate the Go button. Each simulated galaxy starts sending photons towards us, as the expansion of space carries us apart
  • as the simulation runs, it draws a graph showing the distance at which each photon was emitted and the apparent speed we measure when it arrives
  • pause when the furthest galaxy is about halfway across the screen.
  • press the Clear Plot button, and briefly run the simulation again. This acts out our actual situation, of only getting a snapshop of photons from the universe during the brief time of our observations
  • note the slope of the Hubble plot - steep? shallow slope? somewhere in the middle? - and the age of the universe in your simulations
  • Try this again, with a different expansion rate. With a lower expansion rate, is the Hubble plot steeper or shallower, and did the universe take more or less time for the distant galaxy to be carried halfway across the screen? What about with a faster expansion rate?
  • What is the qualitative relationship we see between the slope of the Hubble plot and the age of the universe?

  • note that the graph shows data as it is collected as billions of years go by in the simulation.
