  • Simulations (written in Netlogo, code is available under the Netlogo code tab for each)
    • Simulation of "first-light" spectra from material at a range of distances. This is an illustrative simulation of how light began to be able to travel simultaneously throughout the universe when the expansion cooled matter down to 3000 K (prior to this time, light that was emitted was almost instantly destroyed through interactions with ionized matter). It then shows how, as these first surviving spectra reach the observer, they will be increasingly redshifted (i.e., the first photons in the simulation have only been traveling for about 1 billion years, and so are only slightly redshifted. But after a much greater period of time, the arriving photons will have traveled further and thus arrive more redshifted). The ability to clear the plot allows the user to simulate the experience of a present day observer, who is not able to meausre the photons that arrived in the past or will arrive in the far future, but only those photons that are arriving right now.
