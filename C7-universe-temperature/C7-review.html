Calculating Temperature of the Universe

Read the following text and watch the video to review how to use the spectrum of the cosmic microwave background to calculate the temperature of the universe.

We can use the peak of the spectrum of the cosmic microwave background to calculate the temperature of empty space itself.

  1. Find the peak of the cosmic microwave background spectrum
  2. If reading a graph, drop down to the X-axis to read the wavelength of the peak
  3. Compare that wavelength to the peak wavelength of an object with a known temperature. Calculate the ratio of the two wavelengths. Their temperatures will have the same ratio.
    • if the universe peaks at a longer wavelength, it is cooler than the comparison object
    • if the universe peaks at a shorter wavelength, it is warmer than the comparison object

Use the Background radation simulator (opens in new window) to develop a qualitative sense between the age of the universe and the redshift of the first-light that is reaching us in each era over time

  • set n-galaxies to 2 or 3, making a smaller simulated universe that is easier to examine for patterns. Hit the Setup button
  • activate the Go button. Each simulated galaxy sends:
    1. a vertical line of photons representing the first surviving spectrum, and
    2. starts sending a representative photon (starting out violet, 400 nm) each 500 million years in the simulation
  • when a set of "first-light" photons reach us, that redshifted spectrum is added to the graph below and the simulation pauses. The initial distance of the sending galaxy and the current age of the universe is shown on the left
  • to continue the simulation, hit the Continue button, and then Go
  • over time, what happens to this spectrum that we measure for the first light that traveled through the universe? This is a continuous spectrum, so can be interpreted as a temperature; what is happening to that background temperature over time?
