Using Hubble plots to figure out the history of the universe's expansion

Read the following text and watch the video to review how to use Hubble plots to figure out the history of the expansion of the universe.  

A Hubble - le Maitre plot shows the measured apparent velocity of galaxies versus their distance from us.  This isn't an actual velocity - distant galaxies are not actually moving away from us - but a redshift that we are recording as if it were a velocity, basically by analogy to the Doppler shift seen from moving objects.  What is actually happening is that as space itself stretches and carries galaxies apart from each other, the light that is traveling through that space gets stretched, as well.

During times when space is expanding at a faster rate, the slope of the Hubble plot will be steeper, while during times when the expansion rate is slower, the plot will be less steep.  Combining this with the fact that light will take more time to reach us from further galaxies, we can use observations of galaxies at different distances to figure out how the universe has expanded at different times in the past.  Further galaxies reveal what was happening further in the past, while nearby galaxies show us what has been happening more recently.

Use the Cosmological redshift simulator (opens in new window) to review the relation between distance and lookback time, and how the slope of the Hubble plot indicates the expansion rate of space.

  • use the slider n-galaxies to set the number of galaxies to just 2 or 3, in order to make it easier to see what is going on in the simulation
  • activate the Go button. Each simulated galaxy starts sending photons towards us, as the expansion of space carries us apart
  • which reach us in less time - photons from nearby galaxies, or further galaxies?
  • track the dot representing a single photon as it travels from its galaxy to us. What happens to its wavelength (color) as it travels? Which photons experience more of a change - ones that have travelled shorter or further distances?
  • as the simulation runs, it draws a graph showing the distance at which each photon was emitted and the apparent speed we measure when it arrives (remember this is an apparent speed, not an actual speed; this number comes from converting the redshift to a speed as if it were the Doppler effect). Which photons give the appearance of higher speed - ones from nearby or far away?
  • note that the graph shows data as it is collected as billions of years go by in the simulation. Pause, hit the Clear Plot button, and briefly run the simulation again. This acts out our actual situation, of only getting a snapshop of photons from the universe during the brief time of our observations. What does the graph look like?
  • Now adjust the expansion rate, increase the number of galaxies, hit Setup to clear the simulation, and go through the run-pause-clear-run-pause procedure again to make another snapshot. How does the Hubble plot change if the expansion rate is greater? What about if the expansion rate is slower?
  • act out a model of the history of our universe. Start with the expansion rate very low, and run the simulation until photons are reaching us from a large distance, like 7 or 8 billion light-years. Pause, increase the expansion rate, and run the simulation for a couple of seconds. Pause, clear plot, and run for just a moment, and examine the snapshotHubble plot.
