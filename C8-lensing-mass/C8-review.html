Using gravitational lensing to measure masses

Read the following text and watch the video to review how to use multiple images of distant galaxies caused by gravitational lensing to measure the masses of the lensing clusters.

When light from a distant galaxy is bent in its path - by a massive galaxy cluster bending space - so that we see images in multiple locations, we can use the spacing between those images to measure the mass of the cluster.

  1. Looking at a region of the sky, find multiple images of the same galaxy
  2. Measure the angle between those galaxies, i.e., do they appear far apart or close together?
    • if they appear far apart, the cluster that is between that galaxy and us has a large mass
    • if they appear close together, the cluster that is between that galaxy and us has a small mass

Explore

Use the Gravitational Lensing simulator to quantitatively explore the relationship between the mass of a cluster and the separation of the duplicate images of a distant galaxy.

  • First, explore what we see of a distant galaxy if there is no cluster between us. You can move it closer / further, and off to the left / right. The view from Earth is shown along the bottom of the graphic.
  • Press the "Create Cluster" button to add a galaxy cluster to the simulation between us and the distant galaxy. The simulation calculates the path followed by light that will now reach us, and displays the multiple images as purple in the bottom part of the display.
  • adjust the mass of the intervening cluster to get a sense for how its mass effects the separation of the copies we see of the distant galaxy
